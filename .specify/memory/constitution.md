# FreeMPE Constitution

## Core principles

### I. Human intention is primary

Every Spec Kit turn begins with a human intention: something the person wants
to understand, change, make, preserve, or communicate. A specification must
state that intention in ordinary language before it names a software feature,
device capability, tool, or implementation method.

### II. Choose the smallest fitting route

Every intention specification selects one route: document stewardship, learning,
preservation investigation, or software development. Use the least complex
route that can complete the intention. Do not create software merely because a
software workflow is available.

### III. Specifications define permission and scope

The active intention specification defines what may be done, what must not be
done, the expected artifacts, and how completion will be evaluated. A manual,
task list, suggestion, or curiosity does not expand that scope. When the
intention changes materially, revise or replace the specification before acting.

### IV. Preserve instruments before experimenting

For physical-device work, observation precedes intervention. No experiment may
run until its preservation-investigation specification identifies the device and
host, safety invariants, stop conditions, expected observations, and execution
status `ready to run`. Do not flash firmware, bypass authentication, distribute
proprietary material, or make an irreversible change unless a separately
approved specification establishes both necessity and recovery conditions.

### V. Keep claims, evidence, and interpretation separate

Record observations with their method, relevant environment, and limits. Treat
unknown as a valid result. Do not promote an assumption, community report, or
plausible explanation into a project claim without traceable evidence. Keep
private identifiers and unreviewed raw captures outside version control.

### VI. Make work legible to people

The rendered Quarto Manual is the operational interface for approved work. It
must expose rationale, inputs, procedure, artifacts, checks, execution
boundaries, and recovery or stop conditions. A successful render proves the
manual was built; it does not prove that a physical experiment, transfer, or
other external action occurred.

### VII. Software serves the human intention

Use full Spec Kit specification → plan → tasks → implementation → convergence
only for the software-development route, or when an approved investigation
explicitly produces a software need. For that route, plans and code must trace
back to the human intention and acceptance criteria; tests and convergence must
not be replaced by a polished narrative.

## Route-specific completion

- **Document stewardship** completes with a reviewed, scoped edit and an
  appropriate structural or rendered check.
- **Learning** completes with learner-produced evidence such as an explanation,
  exercise, reflection, or applied demonstration—not merely a generated answer.
- **Preservation investigation** completes with declared observation artifacts,
  an evaluation against the specification, and a decision to stop, revise,
  learn, or begin a new software intention.
- **Software development** completes only after the implementation converges
  with its specification, plan, tasks, and declared validation.

## Governance

This constitution governs project-local Spec Kit artifacts and operational
manuals. Amendments require an explicit human rationale, a review of affected
specifications and manuals, and a version update. The manifesto supplies the
project's ethical context; this document supplies the working discipline.
